If you want to remove an AI watermark from text, first identify the problem you can actually observe. Is text failing to match in search? Does a plain-text field show Markdown symbols? Is the concern about vendor provenance rather than presentation? Those questions lead to different workflows, and a cleanup button cannot answer all of them.

Preserve the original before you start. Paste a working copy into the cleaner and review the findings. Choose the output format that matches your destination, then enable only the categories you need. If your draft includes code, quotations or references, pay particular attention to those sections when comparing the changes.

For the Windows AI text APIs, Microsoft documents a four-character sequence: U+200B U+034F U+2060 U+200B. The invisible-character cleanup can remove this sequence. This is a specific technical case; it should not be generalized into a claim that every AI provider hides characters inside generated prose.

The major vendor systems discussed here encode statistical patterns through word selection. Character cleaning does not remove them. Heavy rewriting can weaken these patterns, but the outcome varies and the text itself changes. NoAITell keeps that distinction explicit: local formatting cleanup and human review are available, while heavy rewriting and vendor watermark verification are not.

After cleaning, read the result in its destination application. Check whether paragraph breaks survived, links still reach their intended pages, and names or numbers remain correct. Then consider the style separately. Replace a vague sentence with a precise observation only when you can verify the new statement, and keep any required acknowledgment of AI assistance.

If your goal is provenance investigation, stop editing the evidence copy and document the known source instead. If your goal is publication, make the prose useful to readers through sound facts, appropriate tone and clear structure. Both workflows benefit from a careful record of changes, but neither should treat a lack of invisible characters as proof about who wrote the document.
