ChatGPT Watermark: EU textGrain Explained

Learn how ChatGPT textGrain differs from invisible characters and clean copied ChatGPT formatting locally.

ChatGPT watermark discussions need a date and a product scope. OpenAI announced its textGrain rollout for eligible EU ChatGPT output in October 2026. The API is different: text watermarking is available by opt-in and remains off by default. A copied ChatGPT answer should not automatically be described as marked simply because it came from an OpenAI product.

OpenAI’s EU provenance announcement and its provenance help article describe a statistical change in word selection. There is no secret character string to strip from ChatGPT prose. Invisible in this context means a reader cannot see the statistical pattern, rather than that a Unicode inspector will reveal an added symbol.

Copying a response can still produce ordinary editing problems. Markdown headings may survive in a plain-text field. A link may contain a known tracking parameter. Spaces or line breaks can come from the clipboard or destination editor. The cleaner below checks these issues locally, without sending your draft to an external processing service.

Choose a publishing format before cleaning. Keep Markdown for a repository document; choose plain text for a form that displays markup literally. After cleaning, compare the result to the source. References, names, dates and numerical claims deserve a separate accuracy check because cleaner formatting does not make generated information reliable.

The tool does not run the textGrain detector, and character removal does not erase its statistical signal. Extensive rewriting can disrupt that signal, with results depending on the passage and method. This distinction matters when someone presents a blank invisible-character report as proof that a document has no provenance marking.

For a useful final edit, replace vague statements with your own observations, remove unnecessary introductions, and read the piece aloud. Keep a source copy if provenance matters to your workflow. Your publishing decision should depend on accuracy, permission and disclosure, rather than on the presence of a dash or a particular transition word.
