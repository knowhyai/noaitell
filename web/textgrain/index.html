textGrain: OpenAI Text Provenance and Editing Limits

Understand textGrain’s EU rollout, API opt-in, and the effect of editing on statistical text provenance.

Local cleaner loading. Your text stays on this device.

TextGrain is OpenAI’s statistical text watermarking technology. It creates a signal through word choices rather than adding hidden Unicode characters. OpenAI announced its EU ChatGPT rollout in October 2026, while API watermarking remains off by default and available by opt-in. These product boundaries matter when discussing whether a particular document might carry the signal.

OpenAI’s technical overview of EU text provenance reports an editing experiment on 400-token passages: replacing 10% of words with synonyms reduced detection from about 92% to 66%, and replacing 25% reduced detection to 17%. These are evaluation results under particular conditions, not success rates for NoAITell or promises for arbitrary documents.

The experiment illustrates why changing words and cleaning characters are different operations. A zero-width-space remover leaves nearly all word choices intact. Extensive rewriting alters the sequence on which a statistical detector relies. It can weaken or disrupt the signal, but it can also alter claims, emphasis and qualifications that the author needed to preserve.

The tool does not call the textGrain detector or rewrite a passage to change its statistical properties. Its category counts describe local rules, not a probability of OpenAI authorship.

For a publication workflow, retain citations and verify claims before improving style. A less generic opening can make a paragraph more useful, but a synonym chosen only to be different may make it less precise. Prefer concrete examples, correct terminology and clear reasoning over arbitrary vocabulary changes. Keep your source version available for comparison.

For provenance review, identify the product, region, model settings and revision history if that information is available. A copied passage rarely preserves all those details. Read detection results with their assumptions and error rates, and do not turn a formatting scan into an origin certificate.
