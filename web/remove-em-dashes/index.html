Remove Em Dashes: Review Punctuation in Context

Normalize em dashes locally, compare edits, and keep punctuation choices separate from AI authorship claims.

Local cleaner loading. Your text stays on this device.

An em dash can interrupt a sentence, add an aside, or introduce an explanation. It is a normal punctuation mark used by human writers. You may still want to remove em dashes when a house style prefers alternatives, a plain-text system handles them poorly, or a draft relies on interruptions so heavily that the argument becomes difficult to follow.

NoAITell lets you inspect punctuation and normalize English punctuation through a selected cleanup option. The current normalization converts em and en dashes to a hyphen, straightens curly quotation marks and expands the single ellipsis character. Because these changes are grouped, review the whole proposed result before copying it into a publication.

A mechanical replacement is not a full sentence edit. Consider “The draft — still incomplete — needs review.” Replacing punctuation alone may leave an awkward aside. You might prefer parentheses or a separate sentence, depending on what you want to emphasize. The cleaner shows a comparison so you can decide whether a manual edit would read better.

Punctuation preferences vary across languages and contexts. Chinese punctuation and quoted material should not be judged by a blanket English style rule. Ranges, negative values, code and technical notation can also depend on particular characters. Keep an untouched source and check these cases whenever you normalize a document for another system.

An em dash is not a vendor watermark, and removing it cannot establish anything about authorship. OpenAI’s provenance documentation describes a statistical word-choice signal, which is a different mechanism. A draft can contain no dashes and still carry a statistical mark. A human author can use many dashes without using an AI system at all.

For a more natural final draft, look at rhythm across the paragraph. Keep an interruption when it helps readers, shorten a crowded sentence when it does not, and avoid changing quotations without indicating the edit. The useful measure is whether your reader follows the point comfortably. Consistent punctuation supports that goal, but it is only one part of careful writing.
