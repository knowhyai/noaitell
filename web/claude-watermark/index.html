Claude watermark searches often mix two questions: whether a model marked its output, and whether a copied answer contains awkward formatting. These are different problems. A paragraph may contain a statistical provenance signal while looking perfectly ordinary. It may also contain bold markers or a tracking link without any connection to provenance.

Anthropic describes global text watermarking for Claude, with nothing added to the text and no hidden characters. Its method changes how plausible next words are selected. See Anthropic’s explanation of Claude text watermarking. Searching for zero-width spaces therefore cannot establish whether a passage carries Claude’s watermark.

The embedded cleaner helps with the editing question. Paste the passage, read the writing-pattern findings, and select only the formatting categories you need. For example, copying a response into an email may leave heading markers and excessive blank lines. Removing these improves presentation without making a claim about how the original passage was generated.

Use the changes view to compare the result with the source. Keep quotations and citations intact, and check any punctuation changes around technical terms. Formulaic introductions can deserve revision, but a familiar expression may fit your intended audience. The tool marks patterns for your judgment rather than silently substituting different claims.

Character cleaning does not remove a statistical watermark. Substantial rewriting can weaken or disrupt a word-choice signal, but it also changes the writing and can change meaning. NoAITell does not perform that kind of rewriting or run Anthropic’s detector. Finding no formatting issues is not a provenance verdict.

If you are investigating a document, keep the original before editing it. Record the source and any known revisions separately from a cleanup copy. For everyday publishing, focus on whether the draft is accurate, specific and readable.
