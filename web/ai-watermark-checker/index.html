AI Watermark Checker: Characters vs Statistical Signals

Check invisible text artifacts locally and understand why a character scan cannot verify vendor statistical watermarks.

An AI watermark checker can mean a character inspector, a vendor provenance detector, or a general writing classifier. These tools answer different questions. NoAITell provides the first kind of inspection alongside writing-pattern review. It does not authenticate model output, identify an author, or return a probability that a passage was generated by AI.

The statistical systems used by major vendors rely on patterns across word choices. OpenAI describes textGrain as such a signal. A scanner that sees only unusual Unicode characters has no basis for declaring that signal present or absent. A familiar phrase or a punctuation habit is also insufficient evidence of a watermark.

There is a distinct character-based case: Microsoft Windows AI text APIs can insert U+200B U+034F U+2060 U+200B. The cleaner recognizes that sequence. Its presence is a character finding, not a complete history of the document. The sequence could have been copied, and the absence of it does not establish human authorship.

To check a draft, paste it into the tool and review category counts. Invisible characters are reported in the summary instead of painted over your text. Read the other findings in context, then choose whether cleanup would help your destination format. The original remains available while you inspect the result and changes.

For a serious provenance question, retain the untouched file and ask which watermark scheme, model configuration and authorized detector are involved. Consider the length of the passage and its editing history. Statistical detection can produce mistakes, and a checker for one provider or media type may not apply to another.

Use a character scan to solve practical issues such as broken search, unexpected cursor movement or copied markup. Use editorial review to improve clarity and check facts. Keep those conclusions separate in any report you share: “invisible characters found” is a reproducible observation, while an assertion about authorship requires a different kind of evidence and appropriate context.
