Common AI tells

  • Em dashes: repeated interruptions can make prose feel formulaic. Review punctuation in context.
  • Scaffold openers: “It is important to note” can often be cut to reach the point sooner.
  • Invisible characters: copy artifacts can interrupt text. Inspect and clean unwanted characters locally.

These are editing cues, not proof of AI authorship.

Capabilities and limits

Free: 3 cleanings per day on this device, up to 500 words each. No login. Scanning and reviewing are free; a cleaning counts when you request a result. Your text is processed in your browser and is never uploaded.

What this tool can do: clean invisible copy artifacts, Markdown residue, excessive whitespace and known tracking parameters. It highlights formulaic expressions for human review. Punctuation alone does not identify an author.

What it cannot do: verify or remove statistical vendor watermarks. Major vendors encode these through word choice, not hidden characters. Heavy rewriting can disrupt these signals, but character cleaning cannot. This app does not perform heavy rewriting.

Among the vendor systems covered here, Microsoft Windows AI text APIs are the character-based exception: Microsoft documents the sequence U+200B U+034F U+2060 U+200B. OpenAI textGrain, Claude watermarking, SynthID Text and Grok API watermarking use statistical signals.

Start with a small sample, inspect the highlighted findings, then open Cleanup options. Keep Markdown if you are publishing to a Markdown editor. Review the changes before copying your result. A clean result means no issues were found by the selected rules; it is not evidence of human authorship.
